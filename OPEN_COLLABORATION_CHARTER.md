# Open Collaboration Charter

## Respect before access
No collaborator is pressured to disclose private notes, unpublished findings, source code, traces, or materials they have not chosen to share.

## Permission before use
Unpublished material is not redistributed, publicly quoted, incorporated into releases, or reused outside the contributor-approved scope.

## Attribution
Material contributions from researchers, preservation projects, and independent implementations must be credited specifically and near the work they support.

## Lawful publication
Copyright, repository licenses, documentation licenses, redistribution terms, and source-site restrictions are respected. If rights are unclear, publication stops until the issue is resolved.

## No proprietary-ROM repository
Publish hashes, provenance, analysis code, reconstruction instructions, and derived factual results rather than proprietary Roland/BOSS firmware or ROMs without clear authorization.

## Transparent AI assistance
AI involvement is disclosed. Machine-assisted analysis is never labeled as physical evidence, primary-source evidence, or independent verification.

## Evidence discipline
Direct VG evidence, exact-chip comparative evidence, family/descendant evidence, software inference, behavioral substitutes, and physical evidence remain separately labeled.

## Validation before fidelity claims
No claim of “1:1”, “bit accurate”, or recovered silicon behavior is made merely because software output is plausible.

## Give back
Lawfully shareable findings, tools, corrections, negative results, and test methods should flow back into the public project rather than only extracting value from prior researchers.

## Commercialization boundary
Any future commercial use must be reviewed against licenses, permissions, attribution commitments, and collaborator expectations. Open collaboration is not a pretext for extracting private work into a product.

## Stop when uncertain
When copyright, licensing, privacy, attribution, or permission is genuinely uncertain, stop and resolve the question before publishing.
